## 1. Patient information

### 1.1 Patient leaflet (CLEAR)
Context: I am a [role] at [setting]. I am creating a patient leaflet about [topic].
Listener: The readers are [who], who may [have limited health literacy / be anxious / be carers].
Expected output: Draft the text for a [length / format] leaflet with question-style headings, short sentences and a reading age of about 11.
Accuracy guardrails: Use only the attached source. Do not include doses. List every clinical claim at the end for me to verify.
Before you write, ask me up to three questions if anything important is unclear.

### 1.2 Patient FAQ from a guideline
Using only the attached guideline, write 10 frequently asked questions and answers for patients about [topic]. Use plain language (reading age about 11). If the guideline does not answer a question, write "Ask your healthcare team".

### 1.3 Simplify technical text
Rewrite the following for patients with limited health literacy. Keep every clinical fact the same. Then list each word you replaced and its replacement.
[paste non-confidential text]

## 2. Teaching

### 2.1 Session plan
Create a [length]-minute teaching session plan for [audience] on [topic], with learning objectives, timings, one interactive activity and a 5-question quiz with answers.

### 2.2 Case scenarios
Create [number] fictional case scenarios for teaching [audience] about [topic]. Include history, a hidden problem, two discussion questions and a model answer. Use invented details only.

### 2.3 Multiple-choice quiz
Using only the attached policy, write [number] multiple-choice questions with four options, one correct answer and a one-sentence explanation citing the policy section.

## 3. Presentations and posters

### 3.1 Presentation outline
I am presenting [topic] to [audience]. I have [time] minutes. Create a slide-by-slide outline (maximum [n] slides) with key-message titles, up to three bullets per slide and a suggested visual.

### 3.2 Abstract to poster
Turn this abstract into A0 poster text: title, background, methods, results, conclusions and a key-message box of up to 20 words. Maximum 600 words.

### 3.3 Waiting-room poster
Write an A3 poster encouraging patients to [behaviour]: headline (max 6 words), subheading (max 15 words), three bullets and a call to action.

## 4. Admin and communication

### 4.1 Professional email
Draft a polite, concise email to [recipient role] about [topic] to [goal]. Under 150 words, ending with a clear request.

### 4.2 Meeting summary (from your own non-confidential notes)
Summarise these notes into: decisions made, actions (owner, deadline) and open questions.

## 5. Review and quality

### 5.1 Accuracy table
List every factual claim in this document in a table: claim, type (dose / statistic / recommendation / other), needs checking against.

### 5.2 Tone check
Highlight sentences that may sound blaming, frightening, patronising or too technical for patients, and suggest kinder alternatives.

### 5.3 Multi-perspective review
Review this from the perspective of (1) [patient], (2) [carer], (3) [senior clinician]. List the top two concerns for each.
