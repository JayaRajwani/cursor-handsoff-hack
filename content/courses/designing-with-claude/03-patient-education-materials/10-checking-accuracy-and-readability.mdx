AI makes drafting fast, which means the **review** becomes the most important part of the process. This lesson gives you a structured way to check any AI-assisted material.

## The four-part quality review

### 1. Clinical accuracy

- Check every clinical claim against your trusted source — guideline, formulary, protocol.
- Look for **subtle errors**: wrong units, outdated thresholds, advice from another country, missing contraindications.
- Check what is **missing** as well as what is wrong. Has an important warning been dropped?
- Verify every **reference**. AI tools can invent realistic-looking citations; confirm each one exists and says what is claimed.

<Prompt title="Create a verification table">

Create a table of every factual statement in this leaflet. Columns: statement, source section in the attached guideline that supports it, and "not found in source" if you cannot find support. Do not change the leaflet.

</Prompt>

Then check the table yourself — Claude's mapping is a helpful starting point, not proof.

### 2. Readability and understanding

- Is the reading level appropriate? Use a readability checker for a precise score.
- Are medical terms explained? Are instructions specific ("Take it with breakfast" rather than "Take it in the morning")?
- **Teach-back test:** ask a lay colleague or patient representative to read it and explain the key actions back to you.

### 3. Equity, inclusion and accessibility

- Does it reflect your patient population's languages, cultures, diets and family structures?
- Is it accessible to people with low vision (font size, contrast), low literacy (pictures, plain words) or disabilities?
- Is there an alternative format available (audio, large print, other languages)?

### 4. Governance

- Has it been approved through the proper process?
- Does it carry a version number, date, review date and owner?
- Is AI assistance recorded where your policy requires?

<Callout type="warning" title="Common AI errors in healthcare materials">

Outdated guideline recommendations · advice from a different country's health system · wrong or mixed units (mmol/L versus mg/dL) · invented statistics or references · lost caveats ("unless your doctor tells you otherwise") · overly confident wording about risks or outcomes.

</Callout>

## Make review part of the workflow

A simple, repeatable sign-off sheet makes reviews consistent. For each piece of material record: title and version, source used, AI tool used, author, clinical reviewer, lay reviewer, approval date and review date. Download the AI safety checklist from lesson 3 and adapt it for your team.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Review is the most important step when AI does the drafting.
- Check clinical accuracy, readability, inclusion and governance — every time.
- Use AI to organise the checking, but do the verification yourself.

</KeyTakeaways>
