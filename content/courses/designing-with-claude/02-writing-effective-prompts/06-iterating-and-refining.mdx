The first response from Claude is a starting point. Professionals get excellent results by **iterating**: reviewing the draft, giving specific feedback and asking for targeted improvements. Claude remembers the conversation, so you can refine step by step without starting again.

## Specific feedback beats "make it better"

Compare these follow-ups:

- ❌ "Make it better."
- ✅ "Shorten section 2 to three bullet points, move 'When to get urgent help' to the top, and replace the word 'hypoglycaemia' with 'low blood sugar' throughout."

Specific feedback is faster and gives you more control. Think like an editor: structure first, then content, then wording, then formatting.

## Useful follow-up prompts

<Prompt title="Ask for options">

Give me three alternative titles for this leaflet: one reassuring, one practical, and one question-style. Keep each under eight words.

</Prompt>

<Prompt title="Ask Claude to critique its own draft">

Review your draft against my original requirements (audience, reading age, length, no doses). List where it falls short, then produce an improved version that fixes those issues.

</Prompt>

<Prompt title="Change the audience">

Now rewrite the same content as a one-page briefing for nursing staff, using appropriate clinical terminology, and add a short "Key points to tell patients" box.

</Prompt>

<Prompt title="Tighten the length">

This is 520 words. Reduce it to 300 words without losing any of the safety information. Show me what you removed.

</Prompt>

## Keep control of versions

When you iterate a lot, it is easy to lose track. Good habits:

- Ask Claude to **number versions** ("Label this Version 3").
- When you are happy with a section, say so: "Keep section 1 exactly as it is from now on."
- **Copy finished text into your own document** (for example Word or Google Docs) regularly. Your document — not the chat — is the master copy.
- If a conversation becomes long and confused, **start a new chat** and paste in the latest good version with a fresh, clear prompt.

## Use "role prompts" for review

You can ask Claude to review a draft from a particular point of view. This is a quick way to spot gaps — though it is not a replacement for real reviewers.

<Prompt title="Multi-perspective review">

Review this discharge advice leaflet from three perspectives: (1) an anxious 70-year-old patient with limited reading skills, (2) their daughter who will be caring for them, and (3) a senior ward nurse checking it for safety. For each perspective, list the top two concerns or questions.

</Prompt>

<Callout type="tip" title="Projects save repetition">

If you regularly create similar materials, consider a Claude Project (where available) containing your style guide, logo guidance and approved template. Claude can then follow your house style without you repeating instructions every time.

</Callout>

<Exercise title="Three rounds of refinement">

Take the leaflet draft from lesson 4 or 5. Give Claude three rounds of specific feedback: one on structure, one on content and one on wording. Save the final version in your own document.

</Exercise>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Treat the first answer as a draft and give specific, editor-style feedback.
- Ask for options, self-critique, audience changes and length targets.
- Keep your own master document and start fresh chats when conversations get messy.

</KeyTakeaways>
