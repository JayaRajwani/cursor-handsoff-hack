Most healthcare professionals give presentations: case discussions, journal clubs, audit results, teaching sessions, management briefings. Claude can help you structure your message, write concise slides, prepare speaker notes and design visuals.

## Start with the message, not the slides

Before opening any slide software, answer three questions — and ask Claude to help if you are stuck:

1. **Who is the audience** and what do they already know?
2. **What is the one thing** they should remember or do afterwards?
3. **How long** do you have, and is there discussion time?

<Prompt title="Presentation outline">

I am presenting the results of a hand-hygiene audit to the hospital's infection control committee. I have 10 minutes plus 5 minutes for questions. The audience are senior clinicians and managers who want to know whether compliance has improved and what we recommend. Create a slide-by-slide outline (maximum 10 slides) with a title for each slide written as a key message, the main content as no more than three bullet points, and a suggested visual. Finish with a clear "decisions needed" slide.

</Prompt>

Notice the instruction to write **slide titles as key messages** ("Compliance improved from 61% to 78% on surgical wards") rather than topics ("Results"). This is one of the most effective changes you can make to any presentation.

## Writing slide content

- **One idea per slide.** If you need more, split the slide.
- **Fewer words.** Slides support you; they are not your script. Put detail in the speaker notes.
- **Show, don't list.** A chart, diagram or image often replaces a paragraph.
- **Consistent design.** Use your organisation's template; keep fonts and colours consistent.

<Prompt title="Speaker notes">

For each slide in this outline, write speaker notes of about 80–100 words in a conversational style, including one transition sentence that leads to the next slide.

</Prompt>

## Turning data into visuals

Claude can suggest the best chart for your data and, if you share non-confidential, aggregated figures, help you build it. For example: "Here are monthly compliance percentages for six wards. Which chart would best show the change over time, and how should I label it so the key message is obvious?"

<Callout type="privacy" title="Only aggregated, non-identifiable data">

Audit and quality data shared with AI tools must be aggregated and non-identifiable — no patient-level or staff-identifiable rows unless your organisation has approved the tool for that use.

</Callout>

## Building the slides

You have several options, depending on your tools and plan:

- **Copy the outline into your template** in PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote — the most reliable approach for formal presentations.
- **Ask Claude to create a slide file** where file creation is available on your plan, then open and polish it in your presentation software.
- **Ask for an HTML slide deck as an artifact** for a quick, browser-based draft.

Whichever you choose, check every number, add references on the slides where needed, and rehearse.

## Preparing for questions

<Prompt title="Anticipate questions">

Here is my presentation outline. List the eight most likely questions or challenges from a sceptical audience of senior clinicians and managers, and suggest a concise, evidence-based answer framework for each (I will add the evidence myself).

</Prompt>

<Exercise title="Outline your next talk">

Use the outline prompt for a real presentation you need to give. Rewrite every slide title as a key message, then generate speaker notes for two slides.

</Exercise>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Decide the audience, the single key message and the time before building slides.
- Write slide titles as key messages; keep one idea per slide and detail in the notes.
- Use only aggregated, non-identifiable data, check every number, and rehearse.

</KeyTakeaways>
