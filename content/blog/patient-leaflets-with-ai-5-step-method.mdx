Patient information leaflets are one of the most useful — and most time-consuming — things healthcare teams produce. A clear leaflet helps patients prepare for procedures, take medicines correctly and know when to seek help. A confusing one is ignored, or worse, misunderstood.

AI assistants such as Anthropic's Claude can cut the time it takes to draft a leaflet from hours to minutes. But speed is only valuable if the result is accurate, readable and safe. Here is a five-step method we teach healthcare professionals.

## Step 1: Plan the leaflet before you write a prompt

Most poor AI output comes from vague requests. Before opening an AI tool, answer five questions:

1. **Who is the reader?** Age, health literacy, language, whether a carer will read it too.
2. **What should they do after reading it?** Leaflets should lead to action.
3. **What are they worried about?** Pain, cost, time off work, what happens next.
4. **What trusted source is the content based on?** A current guideline, protocol or product information that is not confidential.
5. **What format is needed?** A4, folded A5, one page, a digital PDF.

## Step 2: Write a specific, structured prompt

Give the AI your role, audience, desired output and — critically — accuracy guardrails. For example:

> I am a nurse in a day-surgery unit. Using only the attached (non-confidential) pre-operative protocol, draft a one-page leaflet for adults having a day-case procedure under general anaesthetic. Use plain language at a reading age of about 11, question-style headings, and bullet points. Include fasting instructions as written in the protocol, what to bring, and when to call us. Do not add any information that is not in the protocol, and list every clinical instruction at the end so I can verify it.

Two instructions in that prompt do most of the safety work: *"using only the attached protocol"* and *"list every clinical instruction so I can verify it"*.

## Step 3: Refine with specific feedback

Treat the first draft as raw material. Useful follow-ups include:

- "List the ten questions a worried patient might ask and check whether the leaflet answers them."
- "Replace any remaining medical jargon with everyday words, and show me the changes."
- "Highlight any sentences that might sound blaming or frightening."
- "Cut this to 300 words without losing any safety information."

Keep your own master copy in a word processor; the chat is a workspace, not a filing system.

## Step 4: Review properly — this is the most important step

AI tools can produce confident errors: an outdated threshold, advice from another country, a dropped warning, the wrong units. Every leaflet should go through four checks:

- **Clinical accuracy** — every statement checked against the source by a qualified clinician.
- **Readability** — tested with a lay reader; can they explain the key actions back to you?
- **Inclusion and accessibility** — culturally appropriate examples, large fonts, good contrast, simple words.
- **Consistency** — does it match what staff actually tell patients?

## Step 5: Approve, publish and set a review date

Follow your organisation's approval process for patient information. Add a version number, publication date, review date and owning department. If your organisation's policy requires it, record that AI assisted the drafting.

## The golden rule: no patient data

You never need real patient information to write a leaflet. Do not paste case notes, letters or identifiable details into AI tools. If you want an example, invent one.
